# CHANGING SENTENCE / TRANSFORMATION OF SENTENCES

Changing Sentence means changing the form or structure of a sentence without changing its মূল meaning.

Main types:

1. Affirmative ↔ Negative
2. Assertive ↔ Interrogative
3. Assertive ↔ Exclamatory
4. Assertive ↔ Imperative
5. Degree Change
6. Active ↔ Passive
7. Direct ↔ Indirect Speech
8. Simple ↔ Complex ↔ Compound
9. Positive ↔ Comparative ↔ Superlative


==================================================
1. AFFIRMATIVE → NEGATIVE
==================================================

Meaning should remain the same.

Example:

Affirmative:
He is an honest man.

Negative:
He is not a dishonest man.


Another:

Affirmative:
Everyone loves flowers.

Negative:
There is nobody who does not love flowers.


Another:

Affirmative:
Only Rahim can do this.

Negative:
None but Rahim can do this.


Important words:

only → none but
everybody → nobody ... not
always → never ... not
too → so ... that ... not
both → not ... either


Example:

He always tells the truth.

→ He never tells a lie.


==================================================
2. NEGATIVE → AFFIRMATIVE
==================================================

Example:

Negative:
He is not a dishonest man.

Affirmative:
He is an honest man.


Negative:
Nobody hates flowers.

Affirmative:
Everybody loves flowers.


Negative:
He never tells a lie.

Affirmative:
He always tells the truth.


==================================================
3. ASSERTIVE → INTERROGATIVE
==================================================

Assertive = Statement.

Interrogative = Question.

The meaning should remain the same.

Example:

Assertive:
Everybody loves flowers.

Interrogative:
Who does not love flowers?


Another:

Assertive:
Nobody can deny this.

Interrogative:
Who can deny this?


Another:

Assertive:
It is a beautiful place.

Interrogative:
Isn't it a beautiful place?


==================================================
4. INTERROGATIVE → ASSERTIVE
==================================================

Example:

Interrogative:
Who does not love freedom?

Assertive:
Everybody loves freedom.


Interrogative:
Can anyone deny the truth?

Assertive:
Nobody can deny the truth.


Interrogative:
Isn't he a good student?

Assertive:
He is a good student.


==================================================
5. ASSERTIVE → EXCLAMATORY
==================================================

Exclamatory sentence expresses strong emotion.

Common words:

What
How

Example:

Assertive:
It is a very beautiful flower.

Exclamatory:
What a beautiful flower it is!


Assertive:
She is very beautiful.

Exclamatory:
How beautiful she is!


Assertive:
It is a very nice day.

Exclamatory:
What a nice day it is!


Structure:

What + a/an + adjective + noun + subject + verb!


Example:

He is a great man.

→ What a great man he is!


For adjective only:

How + adjective + subject + verb!

She is very beautiful.

→ How beautiful she is!


==================================================
6. EXCLAMATORY → ASSERTIVE
==================================================

Example:

Exclamatory:
What a beautiful flower it is!

Assertive:
It is a very beautiful flower.


Exclamatory:
How beautiful she is!

Assertive:
She is very beautiful.


Exclamatory:
What a great man he is!

Assertive:
He is a very great man.


==================================================
7. ASSERTIVE → IMPERATIVE
==================================================

Imperative expresses:

- Command
- Request
- Advice
- Instruction

Example:

Assertive:
You should obey your parents.

Imperative:
Obey your parents.


Assertive:
You should always speak the truth.

Imperative:
Always speak the truth.


Assertive:
You should not waste your time.

Imperative:
Do not waste your time.


Request:

Assertive:
You are requested to help me.

Imperative:
Please help me.


==================================================
8. IMPERATIVE → ASSERTIVE
==================================================

Example:

Imperative:
Obey your parents.

Assertive:
You should obey your parents.


Imperative:
Do not waste your time.

Assertive:
You should not waste your time.


Imperative:
Please help me.

Assertive:
You are requested to help me.


==================================================
9. DEGREE CHANGE
==================================================

There are 3 degrees:

1. Positive
2. Comparative
3. Superlative


# Positive → Comparative

Positive:

No other boy in the class is as tall as Rahim.

Comparative:

Rahim is taller than any other boy in the class.


# Comparative → Superlative

Comparative:

Rahim is taller than any other boy in the class.

Superlative:

Rahim is the tallest boy in the class.


# Superlative → Positive

Superlative:

Rahim is the tallest boy in the class.

Positive:

No other boy in the class is as tall as Rahim.


Basic Pattern:

Positive:
No other + noun + is as + adjective + as + subject.

Comparative:
Subject + is + comparative + than any other + noun.

Superlative:
Subject + is + the + superlative + noun.


==================================================
10. ACTIVE → PASSIVE
==================================================

Active:

Subject + Verb + Object

Passive:

Object + be + V3 + by + Subject


Example:

Active:
Rahim writes a letter.

Passive:
A letter is written by Rahim.


Another:

Active:
They built the house.

Passive:
The house was built by them.


Another:

Active:
He will do the work.

Passive:
The work will be done by him.


==================================================
11. PASSIVE → ACTIVE
==================================================

Example:

Passive:
A letter is written by Rahim.

Active:
Rahim writes a letter.


Passive:
The house was built by them.

Active:
They built the house.


Passive:
The work will be done by him.

Active:
He will do the work.


==================================================
12. DIRECT → INDIRECT SPEECH
==================================================

Direct Speech:

He said, "I am busy."


Indirect Speech:

He said that he was busy.


Basic changes:

Present Simple → Past Simple
Present Continuous → Past Continuous
Present Perfect → Past Perfect
Past Simple → Past Perfect
will → would
can → could
may → might


Examples:

"I am happy."
→ He said that he was happy.


"I am reading."
→ He said that he was reading.


"I have finished."
→ He said that he had finished.


"I will go."
→ He said that he would go.


Pronoun and time expressions may also change.

now → then
today → that day
tomorrow → the next day
yesterday → the previous day
here → there
this → that
these → those


==================================================
13. SIMPLE → COMPOUND
==================================================

Simple sentence has one main clause.

Compound sentence has two or more independent clauses joined by:

and
but
or
so
yet
for


Example:

Simple:
Being tired, he went to bed.

Compound:
He was tired, so he went to bed.


Simple:
In spite of his poverty, he is honest.

Compound:
He is poor, but he is honest.


==================================================
14. COMPOUND → SIMPLE
==================================================

Example:

Compound:
He was tired, so he went to bed.

Simple:
Being tired, he went to bed.


Compound:
He is poor, but he is honest.

Simple:
In spite of his poverty, he is honest.


==================================================
15. SIMPLE → COMPLEX
==================================================

Complex sentence contains:

Main clause + Subordinate clause


Example:

Simple:
Being ill, he could not attend the class.

Complex:
As he was ill, he could not attend the class.


Simple:
In spite of his poverty, he is honest.

Complex:
Though he is poor, he is honest.


==================================================
16. COMPLEX → SIMPLE
==================================================

Example:

Complex:
As he was ill, he could not attend the class.

Simple:
Being ill, he could not attend the class.


Complex:
Though he is poor, he is honest.

Simple:
In spite of his poverty, he is honest.


==================================================
17. SIMPLE → COMPOUND → COMPLEX
==================================================

Example:

SIMPLE:
Being ill, he could not attend the class.


COMPOUND:
He was ill, so he could not attend the class.


COMPLEX:
As he was ill, he could not attend the class.


Another:

SIMPLE:
In spite of being poor, he is happy.


COMPOUND:
He is poor, but he is happy.


COMPLEX:
Though he is poor, he is happy.


==================================================
18. TOO...TO
==================================================

Structure:

too + adjective + to + V1

Example:

He is too weak to walk.

Meaning:
He is so weak that he cannot walk.


Change:

Too...to
→ so...that...cannot


Example:

He is too weak to walk.

→ He is so weak that he cannot walk.


Another:

The box is too heavy to lift.

→ The box is so heavy that I cannot lift it.


==================================================
19. SO...THAT
==================================================

Example:

He is so weak that he cannot walk.

→ He is too weak to walk.


Structure:

so + adjective + that + cannot

→ too + adjective + to + V1


==================================================
20. AS SOON AS
==================================================

"As soon as" means immediately after.

Example:

As soon as he saw me, he ran away.


Can be changed to:

No sooner had + subject + V3 + than...


Example:

As soon as he saw me, he ran away.

→ No sooner had he seen me than he ran away.


Another form:

Hardly had + subject + V3 + when...


Scarcely had + subject + V3 + when...


Example:

As soon as the teacher arrived, the students became silent.

→ No sooner had the teacher arrived than the students became silent.


==================================================
21. BECAUSE → BECAUSE OF
==================================================

Because + Subject + Verb

Because of + Noun / Gerund


Example:

Because he was ill, he could not attend the class.

→ Because of his illness, he could not attend the class.


Another:

Because it rained, we stayed home.

→ Because of the rain, we stayed home.


==================================================
22. IN SPITE OF → THOUGH / ALTHOUGH
==================================================

In spite of + noun/gerund

Though/Although + subject + verb


Example:

In spite of his poverty, he is honest.

→ Though he is poor, he is honest.


In spite of being tired, he continued working.

→ Though he was tired, he continued working.


==================================================
23. IF → UNLESS
==================================================

Unless = if...not


Example:

If you do not study, you will fail.

→ Unless you study, you will fail.


If you do not hurry, you will miss the train.

→ Unless you hurry, you will miss the train.


IMPORTANT:

Unless + affirmative clause

Unless you study... ✅

Unless you do not study... ❌


==================================================
24. AND → BOTH...AND
==================================================

Example:

Rahim and Karim are good students.

→ Both Rahim and Karim are good students.


==================================================
25. EITHER...OR
==================================================

Used when there are two alternatives.

Example:

Rahim or Karim will come.

→ Either Rahim or Karim will come.


==================================================
26. NEITHER...NOR
==================================================

Used to express two negative alternatives.

Example:

Rahim does not come.
Karim does not come.

→ Neither Rahim nor Karim comes.


==================================================
27. NOT ONLY...BUT ALSO
==================================================

Used to connect two related ideas.

Example:

He is intelligent and hardworking.

→ He is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

==================================================
GOLDEN RULE
==================================================

While changing a sentence:

1. Keep the original meaning.
2. Change the grammatical structure.
3. Maintain the correct tense.
4. Maintain subject-verb agreement.
5. Use correct pronouns.
6. Do not change the মূল meaning.
7. Check punctuation.
